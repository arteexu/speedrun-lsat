# LR Flaws Primer (original source for AI card generation)

Original, license-clean teaching notes on common Logical Reasoning flaw types.
This is the single named source the AI card generator draws from; generated items
cite it as their source.

## Causal flaws
- Correlation treated as causation: concluding X causes Y from co-occurrence alone, without ruling out coincidence, a common cause, or reverse causation.
- Reversed causation: treating the effect as the cause.
- Common cause overlooked: a third factor produces both X and Y.
- Post hoc: inferring causation merely because one event followed another in time.

## Conditional flaws
- Necessary vs sufficient confusion: treating a necessary condition as if it were sufficient (or vice versa).
- Mistaken reversal: affirming the consequent (if A then B; B, therefore A).
- Mistaken negation: denying the antecedent (if A then B; not A, therefore not B).

## Sampling and evidence flaws
- Unrepresentative sample: generalizing from a biased or too-small sample.
- Appeal to ignorance: treating the absence of evidence against a claim as evidence for it.

## Scope and quantity flaws
- Part-to-whole: assuming what is true of the parts is true of the whole.
- Percent vs number: confusing a proportion with an absolute count.
- Equivocation: shifting the meaning of a key term within the argument.

## Structural flaws
- Circular reasoning: assuming the conclusion among the premises.
- Ad hominem: attacking the source rather than the argument.
- Straw man: distorting an opponent's position into a weaker one.
- False dilemma: presenting only two options when others exist.
- Appeal to inappropriate authority or emotion.
